\section{Decision Tree Results}

This section presents the performance analysis of the Decision Tree classifier using a custom scratch implementation. 
Three resampling strategies were evaluated in order to address class imbalance: SMOTE, Tomek links, and SMOTE-Tomek. 
All models were trained using the Gini impurity criterion and evaluated on separate training, validation, and test sets.

\subsection{Scratch Implementation Results}

The table below summarizes the accuracy obtained with the scratch Decision Tree using a fixed maximum depth of 5.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Decision Tree (Scratch) Performance Across Resampling Strategies}
\label{tab:dt_scratch_results}
\begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Resampling} & \textbf{Training Acc.} & \textbf{Validation Acc.} & \textbf{Test Acc.} & \textbf{Class 1 Behavior} \\ \hline
SMOTE & 0.8473 & 0.7958 & 0.7970 & High recall, low precision \\ \hline
Tomek & 0.9558 & 0.9408 & 0.9500 & Higher precision, low recall \\ \hline
SMOTE-Tomek & 0.8507 & 0.7955 & 0.7970 & Recall = 0.84, Precision = 0.20 \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

For the SMOTE-Tomek dataset, the training set was perfectly balanced with 15,133 samples per class. 
Despite this balance, the model still exhibited a strong bias toward recall for the minority class (Class 1), achieving a recall of 0.84 but a low precision of 0.20. 
This behavior indicates that while the model successfully detects most rare events, it also generates a significant number of false positives. 
The relatively close values between training and test accuracy suggest limited overfitting at this depth.

\subsection{Scikit-Learn Implementation}

For comparison purposes, the Decision Tree classifier was also trained using the scikit-learn implementation. 
The table below reports the training time and F1-scores for each class.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Scikit-Learn Decision Tree Performance Across Resampling Strategies}
\label{tab:dt_sklearn_results}
\begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Resampling} & \textbf{Training Time (s)} & \textbf{Training Acc.} & \textbf{Validation Acc.} & \textbf{Class 0 F1} & \textbf{Class 1 F1} \\ \hline
SMOTE & 2.25 & 0.9374 & 0.8749 & 0.93 & 0.39 \\ \hline
Tomek & 0.59 & 0.9681 & 0.9382 & 0.97 & 0.38 \\ \hline
SMOTE-Tomek & 2.20 & 0.9422 & 0.8818 & 0.93 & 0.39 \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Compared to the scratch implementation, the scikit-learn Decision Tree achieved higher overall accuracy and significantly faster training times. 
However, the F1-score for the minority class remained relatively low across all resampling strategies, confirming that class imbalance remains a challenging aspect of the task even with optimized libraries.
