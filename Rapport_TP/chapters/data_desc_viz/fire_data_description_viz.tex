\section{Fire data description and visualization}

This section details the initial loading, cleaning, and exploratory data analysis (EDA) performed on the raw fire datasets for Algeria and Tunisia, culminating in several key visualizations of the combined data.

\subsection{Data Loading and Inspection}
The analysis began by loading the separate VIIRS (Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite) fire detection files for Algeria (\texttt{df\_fire\_dza}) and Tunisia (\texttt{df\_fire\_tun}).

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Initial Check:} Descriptive statistics (\texttt{describe()}) were computed to understand the distribution, range, and central tendencies of numerical features like $\texttt{frp}$ and brightness temperatures.
    \item \textbf{Data Quality:} Inspection of data types, memory usage, and the percentage of missing values (\texttt{isnull().mean()}) confirmed data integrity and structure consistency across both datasets. Crucially, the missing value analysis revealed low data sparsity, making immediate imputation unnecessary.
    \item \textbf{Data Integration:} The two datasets were merged into a single GeoDataFrame, $\texttt{df\_fire}$, using the \texttt{pd.concat()} function, ready for combined regional analysis.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Variable Definitions and Geospatial Metrics}

Understanding the physical meaning of the VIIRS variables is critical for interpreting the visualizations. The following table summarizes key columns used in the analysis:

    \begin{tabular}{llcl}
    \toprule

    \midrule
    \texttt{bright\_ti4} & Brightness temperature, Band M13 (4 $\mu\text{m}$) & Kelvin (K) & Highly sensitive to hot fires. \\
    \texttt{bright\_ti5} & Brightness temperature, Band M15 (11 $\mu\text{m}$) & Kelvin (K) & Used for background temperature reference. \\
    \texttt{frp} & Fire Radiative Power & Megawatts (MW) & Measures fire intensity and biomass combustion rate. \\
    \texttt{scan} & Pixel size in scan (E–W) direction & Degrees & Pixel width, increases with distance from nadir. \\

    \bottomrule

\end{table}

The distributions of the numerical columns were visualized using histograms (Figure \ref{fig:histograms}), revealing heavily right-skewed distributions for intensity variables like $\texttt{frp}$, indicating that most fire detections are of low power, with few extreme events.

\begin{figure}[!h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.5\textwidth]{images/Fire/heatmaps of numerical.png}
    \caption{Heatmap of the Pearson correlation matrix for numerical variables. High correlation exists between the brightness temperatures and the pixel dimensions.}
    \label{fig:heatmap}
\end{figure}

A correlation matrix heatmap (Figure \ref{fig:heatmap}) was generated to explore relationships between the numerical features. Key findings include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A very high correlation (Spearman $\rho \approx 0.96$) was observed between $\texttt{bright\_ti4}$ and $\texttt{bright\_ti5}$, which is expected as both measure thermal radiation from the same source, but in different bands.
    \item Positive correlations were also noted between $\texttt{bright\_ti4}$, $\texttt{frp}$, $\texttt{scan}$, and $\texttt{track}$, suggesting that larger or more intense fires (higher $\texttt{frp}$) often have higher brightness temperatures and are more likely to be detected in larger, more distorted pixels (higher $\texttt{scan}$ and $\texttt{track}$ values).
\end{itemize}

Boxplots (Figure \ref{fig:boxplots}) further confirmed the presence of significant outliers in the fire intensity metric $\texttt{bright\_ti5}$ and $\texttt{frp}$), due to the presence of extremely hot and large fire events.

\subsection{Final Data Preparation}

The processed $\texttt{df\_fire}$ GeoDataFrame, now including the $\texttt{datetime}$ and $\texttt{hour}$ columns, was saved as $\texttt{fire\_df.csv}$ for use in subsequent advanced modeling stages.
